Nonprofit Policy Builder

Answer a few questions about how your organization works, and six plain-English security policies and six forms are tailored to match your answers. Change an answer and every document updates instantly. Built for small nonprofits, including organizations with volunteers, shared logins and frequent turnover. Your answers stay in your browser and are never saved or sent anywhere.

Not legal advice. These are starting templates. Adjust them to fit your organization, have your board approve them, and ask a lawyer to review them, especially your state’s breach notification and record retention rules.

Before you start: what to know and what to gather (2-minute read)

Free, private, nothing to install

  • No sign-up, download or login. It's free for nonprofits to use.
  • Nothing you type is sent anywhere. There's no server, no accounts and no tracking. The app makes no network calls and loads nothing from other sites.

Your answers are not saved

Everything you type stays in this browser tab only. Closing or refreshing the tab erases it. Plan to finish in one sitting, or copy or print what you have before you stop.

  • Pin this tab so it's harder to close by accident: right-click the tab and choose "Pin".
  • If you try to close or refresh the page after typing, your browser will ask you to confirm first.
  • Printing opens in a separate tab. Print from there, then close that tab. Your answers will still be here.

Gather this first

For the questions

  • Organization name
  • Policy owner's name and title
  • Primary IT contact's name, phone and email
  • Alternate incident contact's name, title, phone and email
  • Board chair's name
  • Where printed emergency copies will be kept
  • Email platform, donor database, password manager and backup service
  • Yes/no: paid staff, volunteers, organization devices, personal devices, shared logins, high turnover, online donations, card payments, outside tech support, cyber insurance, backup service, office server or storage device, office Wi-Fi, client records, paper client records

For the Incident Contact List

  • Executive director, board chair and communications contact, with office and after-hours phones
  • Bank fraud line and the last 4 digits of the account
  • Cyber insurer's claims line and policy number
  • IT support and managed support providers, domain registrar, website host and developer, cloud storage, and legal counsel
  • Local FBI field office, police non-emergency line and state attorney general's office

You can also write these on the printed sheet by hand.

The six policies work as a set

The policies refer to each other. For example, Acceptable Use points to the Passwords & MFA Policy, and the Offboarding Checklist points to the Security Awareness and Training Policy. If you don't adopt a policy, the others may still mention it, so edit those references after you copy them. The framework mappings assume you adopt all six.

Based on recognized security standards

Each document is linked to the parts of two trusted frameworks that it supports: the CIS Controls from the Center for Internet Security (Implementation Group 1, the starter set for small organizations) and the from the U.S. National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST). That helps show a board, funder or insurer that your policies follow recognized guidance. Using them does not make you certified or compliant, because a policy only says what you plan to do. What counts is following it day to day. You may also have to meet other rules, such as state privacy laws or grant requirements.

How to use this page

  1. Delete the sample answers and type in your own or leave blank.
  2. Fill this out once, down to the last question. All twelve documents fill in for you as you type. Every question is optional, so skip any you can't answer and fill in the blank later.
  3. Print or copy. Use "Print" or "Copy" for the document on screen, or "More" for some or all of them.
  4. Fill in any blanks. Some details aren't asked for here, so add them after you copy the documents into your own files. Add or remove table rows there as needed.

You can't edit documents on this page. To make changes, copy and paste them into Word or Google Docs, or print to PDF and use a PDF editor.
