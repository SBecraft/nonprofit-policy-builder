Framework mappings

Each policy is labelled with the security frameworks it supports. This lets your board, a funder, an insurer or an auditor see that the policies follow recognized standards, not just someone's opinion.

To give these mappings to your board or keep them with your records, use the Framework Summary tab in the policy builder. It prints on one page with your organization's name, and you can add it to the policies with More.

The six policies are designed as a set, and the mappings assume you adopt all six. If you leave one out, the parts of the frameworks it covers are no longer covered, and the other policies may still mention it, so edit those references after you copy them.

, Implementation Group 1

The CIS Controls, from the Center for Internet Security, are 18 groups of practical security steps, listed in priority order. Implementation Group 1 (IG1) is the starting set, the "essential cyber hygiene" meant for small organizations with limited IT help. A label like CIS 5 · Accounts means the policy supports CIS Control 5.

The

The NIST Cybersecurity Framework, from the U.S. National Institute of Standards and Technology, sorts security work into six functions: Govern, Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond and Recover. A label like NIST PR.AA means the Protect function, Identity Management, Authentication and Access Control category.

Your answers and privacy

Nothing is saved

  • Your answers exist only in the open browser tab while you use it.
  • The app does not use cookies, local storage or any other browser storage.
  • Closing the tab, refreshing it or leaving the page erases your answers. If you try to close or refresh after typing, your browser will ask you to confirm.
  • To keep your work, copy the documents into Word or Google Docs, or print them, before you close the tab.

Tip: pin the tab (right-click the tab and choose "Pin") so it's harder to close by accident.

Nothing is sent over the internet

  • No backend. The site is hosted on Cloudflare, which stores the app's files and sends them to your browser. That's all Cloudflare does: no code runs on a server, and there's no database, accounts or logins. Once the page loads, the app runs entirely in your browser.
  • Your answers stay in your browser. The app makes no network requests after the page loads, so nothing you type is sent to Cloudflare, to us or to anyone else.
  • What Cloudflare sees. Like any web host, Cloudflare receives a standard request for each file it sends: your IP address, your browser type, which file and when. It never receives anything you type.
  • No outside services. The app loads no outside fonts, analytics, trackers or ads. Many sites get their fonts from services such as Google Fonts, which lets that company see every visitor's IP address. This site uses fonts already on your computer, so no other company learns that you visited.
  • Locked down by design. The site's security settings block outgoing connections and form submissions, so even a later change to the code could not quietly send your answers anywhere.

What the web host keeps

  • What the host keeps. Cloudflare, which hosts this site, keeps standard server logs of each request, including your IP address, under its own privacy policy (www.cloudflare.com/privacypolicy). This site adds no cookies, analytics or tracking scripts, so Cloudflare sees only that a file was requested, never what you type.
  • Cloudflare acts as a service provider. Its privacy policy says it processes visitors' data on behalf of the sites it serves, and that it doesn't sell or rent personal information. The interest-based ads it mentions are for visitors to Cloudflare's own website, not to the sites it hosts, like this one.
  • How long the logs are kept. Cloudflare's privacy policy gives no set time: it says data is kept as long as its business purposes need. Cloudflare's blog says that for most sites it discards access logs within 4 hours (blog.cloudflare.com/what-cloudflare-logs).
  • What Cloudflare adds, and doesn't. Every page is sent over HTTPS, so no one in between can read or change it. Cloudflare adds no branding, ads or analytics to the pages.
  • For the most privacy, use the downloaded copy. After you download it, the app runs from your own computer and never contacts a web host. (GitHub, where you download it, sees that one download request.) See Download the app.

After you print, copy or save

Once you print, copy or save, your devices take over. This app can’t protect documents after they leave this page. Print and save only on a trusted network, such as your organization’s network or a secured home network, not public Wi-Fi. Use a printer and storage you trust. Shared printers and folders that copy to the cloud, like OneDrive, Google Drive or Dropbox, may keep a copy. Pick up printouts right away, and save only to your organization’s account, not a personal one. Treat these documents like any other internal record.

Check it yourself

  1. Open your browser's developer tools (F12, or right-click and choose "Inspect").
  2. Go to the Network tab. You'll see the page's own files, which loaded when it opened. Click the clear button (🚫 in Chrome and Edge, a trash can in Firefox) to empty the list, then fill in the questionnaire. No new requests appear. If you see entries from a browser extension or a blocked (red) script from the web host, those aren't the app: try a private window, where extensions are usually off.
  3. Go to the Application tab (or Storage in Firefox). The site has no cookies and nothing in local or session storage.

Search engines and AI tools

  • Search engines and AI search tools may list this site, so other nonprofits can find it.
  • Companies that build AI models are asked not to copy this site's pages, including the policy and form wording, to train their AI.
  • These programs only ever see the page with its sample answers. They can never see your answers, because your answers never leave your browser.

Sharing the link

  • When you share this site's link on LinkedIn, Facebook, Teams, Slack, Discord or in a text message, a preview card appears with a picture, a title and a short description. Hidden tags in the page (called Open Graph tags) set what the card shows.
  • The card is built from the site's public page, the same one anyone can open. It never includes your answers.
  • The tags load nothing, track nothing and send nothing about you. The picture comes from this site, not from another company.

Printing and copying

  • Print opens the document you're viewing in a separate tab and starts printing there. That tab closes by itself when you finish or cancel printing; your answers stay in the original tab. While the print tab is open, the original tab shows a “Printing in Progress” message and the documents can’t be used, because browsers won’t copy while the print tab is open. The message goes away by itself when the print tab closes. If your browser blocks new tabs, it prints from the same tab instead.
  • More lets you print or copy any combination of the twelve documents. Each printed document starts on a new page.
  • Sample answers are caught first. If a document you print or copy still has one of the sample answers the page came with, such as “Riverbend Community Pantry (example)”, a box lists them. Choose Go back and change to fix them, or Print anyway or Copy anyway to carry on. Nothing is saved or sent.
  • Copying keeps headings, tables and other formatting when you paste into Word or Google Docs.
  • If Copy is blocked. Some browsers block copying. If that happens, the document is selected for you: press Ctrl+C (Cmd+C on a Mac) to copy it, then paste it as usual.
  • To save a PDF, choose "Save as PDF" in the print dialog.
  • When you print, copy or save, keeping the documents safe is up to you. This app can’t protect them once they leave this page. Use a trusted network (not public Wi-Fi), a printer you trust and your organization’s storage. Shared printers and folders that copy to the cloud, like OneDrive, Google Drive or Dropbox, may keep a copy. Pick up printouts right away, and save only to your organization’s account, not a personal one.
  • Each form fits on one printed page, except the Incident Contact List, which takes two. Print it double-sided so both pages stay together. The Incident Response Plan may run onto a second page. The Asset Inventory prints in landscape.

The Incident Contact List is confidential. It may hold personal phone numbers and insurance details. Give it only to people who need it during an incident, and keep a printed copy somewhere easy to reach in case email or files are locked.

Works on any device

  • Computers, tablets and phones. The app works in any up-to-date browser. On a phone or a narrow window, the questions sit above the document and the whole page scrolls, and wide tables turn into stacked cards. It has been checked on an iPhone. For printing and editing the documents, a computer is easier.
  • Light and dark mode. Both pages follow your device's light or dark setting. Printed documents always use dark text on a white background.
  • Accessibility. Every question has a label. The document tabs work with the arrow keys, Home and End. The highlight switch and the "Watch larger" button tell screen readers whether they are on, and pop-up boxes keep keyboard focus until they close. The app has not yet been formally tested for accessibility (with a screen reader or against the WCAG guidelines). If something doesn't work for you, please report it (see Report a problem).

Security

The app is a security tool, so it is built to follow the practices it recommends.

Protections built in

  • Typed text can't run as code. Everything you type is displayed as plain text, so a malicious entry can't turn into a script (cross-site scripting).
  • Strict security policy. The site runs only its own two script files, listed by name. Any other script, including one injected into the page, is refused, outgoing connections are blocked, and forms can't be submitted anywhere.
  • Can't be hidden inside another website. The app refuses to load inside another site's frame, which blocks "clickjacking" tricks.
  • No third-party code. No outside scripts, fonts or libraries, so there is nothing outside the site that could be tampered with to change the app.
  • What the web host adds. The site's host, Netlify, adds a short comment and a tag for its "Powered by Netlify" badge script to every page it serves. Because the security policy lists the app's own script files by name, browsers refuse to load the badge script, so it never runs. The downloaded copy has neither.
  • Encrypted connection. The live site is served only over HTTPS.

Testing

The app is tested with:

  • security header and HTTPS scans
  • automated vulnerability scanning
  • a source code review
  • manual attempts to inject scripts into every text field
  • a check for third-party code (dependencies)
  • automatic code scanning each time the code changes

Done (November 2026):

  • Dependency check. The app uses no third-party code, libraries, fonts or packages, so there are no outside parts to scan for weaknesses or keep up to date.
  • Source code scan. Semgrep, an automated code scanner, ran 130 security rules and found nothing. Every place the code adds text to the page was also checked by hand: everything you type is made safe first.
  • Automatic code scanning. GitHub's CodeQL scanner checks the app's code each time it changes, and once a week. Its first scan found nothing. GitHub also warns if an outside package with known weaknesses is ever added (there are none today), and blocks passwords and keys from being uploaded by mistake.
  • Script injection. Five well-known attack strings were typed into each of the 14 text boxes and checked in all twelve documents, the print tab and copied text. None ran; each one showed up as plain text.
  • Automated attack scan. OWASP ZAP, a free security scanner, attacked a copy of the app on a test computer and found no weaknesses in the app.
  • AI-assisted code review. A second review of the code found no problems. It did suggest three small ways to tighten the site's security settings. Two have been made. The third was reviewed and left as it is, because the risk is very low and changing it could break the printed layouts. All three were re-tested on the live site, and the two changes are in place.
  • Security settings on the live site. Four free scanners checked the site's security settings and its encrypted connection. It scored A+ on SecurityHeaders.com, Mozilla HTTP Observatory and Qualys SSL Labs, and Google's CSP Evaluator found no serious problems.
  • Live site scan. OWASP ZAP looked over the live site without attacking it and found nothing new.
  • Browser checks on the live site. The page loads only its own files, sends nothing while you fill it in, stores nothing (no cookies or saved data) and forgets your answers when the tab closes. It refuses scripts it doesn't know, and it can't be shown inside another website.

All the earlier code checks were repeated after the changes, on a test computer, and found nothing new. One suggestion from these tests, a newer browser setting called Trusted Types, was reviewed and left as it is. It would add another layer of protection to how the page shows what you type, but it doesn't point to a current weakness: the app already makes everything you type safe.

Using a downloaded copy? If you open the app from your own computer instead of the website, the site's security settings don't apply, because they are sent by the web host. The app still never saves your answers or sends them over the internet.
