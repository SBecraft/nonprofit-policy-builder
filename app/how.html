Framework mappings

Each policy is labelled with the security frameworks it supports. This lets your board, a funder, an insurer or an auditor see that the policies follow recognized standards, not just someone's opinion.

To give these mappings to your board or keep them with your records, use the Framework Summary tab in the policy builder. It prints on one page with your organization's name, and you can add it to the policies with More.

The six policies are designed as a set, and the mappings assume you adopt all six. If you leave one out, the parts of the frameworks it covers are no longer covered, and the other policies may still mention it, so edit those references after you copy them.

, Implementation Group 1

The CIS Controls, from the Center for Internet Security, are 18 groups of practical security steps, listed in priority order. Implementation Group 1 (IG1) is the starting set, the "essential cyber hygiene" meant for small organizations with limited IT help. A label like CIS 5 · Accounts means the policy supports CIS Control 5.

The

The NIST Cybersecurity Framework, from the U.S. National Institute of Standards and Technology, sorts security work into six functions: Govern, Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond and Recover. A label like NIST PR.AA means the Protect function, Identity Management, Authentication and Access Control category.

Alignment, not certification. The labels show which parts of each framework a policy supports. Using these policies does not make an organization "compliant" or "certified". Following them in practice is what reduces risk. The wording and mappings are reviewed once a year.

Your answers and privacy

Nothing is saved

  • Your answers exist only in the open browser tab while you use it.
  • The app does not use cookies, local storage or any other browser storage.
  • Closing the tab, refreshing it or leaving the page erases your answers. If you try to close or refresh after typing, your browser will ask you to confirm.
  • To keep your work, copy the documents into Word or Google Docs, or print them, before you close the tab.

Tip: pin the tab (right-click the tab and choose "Pin") so it's harder to close by accident.

Nothing is sent anywhere

  • The app has no server, no database, no accounts and no logins.
  • It makes no network calls, and nothing you type ever leaves your computer.
  • It loads no outside fonts, analytics, trackers or ads. Many sites get their fonts from services such as Google Fonts, which lets that company see every visitor's IP address. This site uses fonts already on your computer, so no other company learns that you visited.
  • The site's security settings block outgoing connections and form submissions, so even a later change to the code could not quietly send your answers anywhere.

Like every website, the host that delivers the page sees your IP address. It never sees anything you type.

Check it yourself

  1. Open your browser's developer tools (F12, or right-click and choose "Inspect").
  2. Go to the Network tab, then fill in the questionnaire. No new requests appear.
  3. Go to the Application tab (or Storage in Firefox). The site has no cookies and nothing in local or session storage.

Search engines and AI tools

  • Search engines and AI search tools may list this site, so other nonprofits can find it.
  • Companies that build AI models are asked not to copy this site's pages, including the policy and form wording, to train their AI.
  • These programs only ever see the page with its sample answers. They can never see your answers, because your answers never leave your browser.

Printing and copying

  • Print opens the document you're viewing in a separate tab and starts printing there. Close that tab when you're done; your answers stay in the original tab. If your browser blocks new tabs, it prints from the same tab instead.
  • More lets you print or copy any combination of the twelve documents. Each printed document starts on a new page.
  • Copying keeps headings, tables and other formatting when you paste into Word or Google Docs.
  • To save a PDF, choose "Save as PDF" in the print dialog.
  • The Incident Response Plan and each form fit on one printed page, except the Incident Contact List, which takes two. Print it double-sided so both pages stay together. The Asset Inventory prints in landscape.

The Incident Contact List is confidential. It may hold personal phone numbers and insurance details. Give it only to people who need it during an incident, and keep a printed copy somewhere easy to reach in case email or files are locked.

Security

The app is a security tool, so it is built to follow the practices it recommends.

Protections built in

  • Typed text can't run as code. Everything you type is displayed as plain text, so a malicious entry can't turn into a script (cross-site scripting).
  • Strict security policy. The site only runs its own script files. Scripts injected into the page are refused, outgoing connections are blocked, and forms can't be submitted anywhere.
  • Can't be hidden inside another website. The app refuses to load inside another site's frame, which blocks "clickjacking" tricks.
  • No third-party code. No outside scripts, fonts or libraries, so there is nothing outside the site that could be tampered with to change the app.
  • Encrypted connection. The live site is served only over HTTPS.

Testing

Before launch, the app is tested with:

  • security header and HTTPS scans
  • automated vulnerability scanning
  • a source code review
  • manual attempts to inject scripts into every text field

Testing status: planned. Results will be summarized here once testing is complete.

Using a downloaded copy? If you open the app from your own computer instead of the website, the site's security settings don't apply, because they are sent by the web host. The app still makes no network calls and saves nothing.
